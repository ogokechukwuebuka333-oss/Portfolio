Activation Rescue

From days of waiting to instant access. New learners on Dataset Nexus Tech's learning platform went through a manual, sales-led onboarding before they could start a course. I redesigned it into self-service.

The problem

Interested learners had to wait days, and go through the sales team, before they could start.

My approach

Ask only for what's needed to get someone learning, and let the product, not a person, give access.

The result

Self-service onboarding with instant access, and a sign-up form cut from more than 15 fields to two.

Business and user problem

Interest peaked at sign-up, then the wait began

Getting a learner into a course depended on the sales team. Someone filled in a long form, then waited while the team followed up and set up their access by hand. That took days, and every new learner needed a person's time before they could start.

For the business

Growth tied to people's time

Every enrolment went through the sales team, so more sign-ups meant more manual work, not more learners.

For the learner

A form, then silence

More than 15 fields to fill in, then days before they could open a lesson. The moment they were most interested was the moment they had to wait.

Constraints and trade-offs

Asking for less meant knowing less up front

The long form existed for a reason: it told the team who was signing up. Cutting it to two fields was a deliberate trade, which I called a "minimal viable data" approach: ask only for what's needed to give someone access, and nothing more at the door.

What we gave up

Detail about each learner at the moment they signed up.

What we gained

Nothing standing between an interested person and their first lesson.

Say what happened to the information the old form collected: was it asked later, dropped, or gathered another way? Add any technical or business constraints (payment, course access rules, the LMS itself).

Key decisions

Four calls that shaped the new onboarding

  1. Let the product give access, not a person

    The flow grants access automatically the moment sign-up is complete, so no one waits on the sales team to start learning.

  2. Two fields at the door

    Email and password only. Everything else the old form asked for stopped being a condition of getting in.

  3. Design for startup builders too

    The discovery finding fed into how the platform talks to new learners, how onboarding is framed and how the community is set up.

  4. One design system for product and marketing

    Shared components across the platform and the marketing pages, so what people saw before signing up matched what they got after, and handoff to development had less friction.
