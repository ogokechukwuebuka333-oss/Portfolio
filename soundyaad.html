SY-03 · Music tech · Activation · Product strategy

Soundyaad

Artists signed up, then nothing happened. Soundyaad helps independent African artists store, distribute and promote their music. Sign-ups were healthy. Almost nobody got as far as using the product.

The problem

Most artists never used the product after signing up, and the dashboard couldn't show where they stopped.

My approach

Count it by hand from the admin data, find the cause, then split the fix between me, product and leadership.

Where it is

A written recommendation with owners, an eight-week sequence and targets, shared for discussion. Not yet in motion.

What I owned

  • The activation diagnosis, from the admin analytics hub and a live test sign-up
  • A proposed single definition of "active" and the measures to judge the work by
  • The recommendation, split into three tracks with three owners
  • Three explainer videos showing artists how to use the core tools
Business and user problem

An activation problem, not a churn problem

Artists weren't leaving after getting value. Most never got any. The tools aren't hard: a smart link takes minutes and the Vault takes one upload. But the product was explained exactly once, in a welcome email, and never again.

For the business

Sign-ups that go nowhere

Sign-ups and profile completion were healthy. Everything broke at the point where the product was supposed to start working for the artist.

For the artist

They did the work, and got silence

Artists who made a smart link mostly saw no fans and no clicks, so they had no reason to come back and make another.

Evidence

What the numbers said

Counted by hand from the admin dashboard, covering direct self-serve artists only. Shown as ratios: the figures were for an internal discussion, and they were still being confirmed against a data export.

  1. Using the product produced no visible result

    Most artists who made a smart link had zero fans. They did what we asked and nothing happened, so they didn't come back.

  2. A smart link only works if it's shared, and nobody said how

    Artists posted once and stopped. We'd never sent guidance on where to share or how often.

  3. Two of the four tools were never mentioned

    The welcome email skipped the artist page and curator pitching, the one artists pay for. A few artists were holding paid pitch credits they'd never used.

  4. The one email was written for the wrong artist

    It opened with steps to launch "your next release", but about five in six artists had no next release. We recruit emerging artists and welcomed them like signed acts.

  5. We couldn't see where artists gave up

    The product analytics tab reported zero across the board. Every fix was a hypothesis until that changed.

Constraints

Three different answers to "how many artists are active?"

The dashboard defined "active" three ways across its tabs, and one counted recent logins. In a week where nothing was sent and nothing shipped, "active" still went up, because every new sign-up counted as active for a while. Anyone reading that number would have seen progress that didn't happen.

Proposed definition

An artist is active if they took an action on any of the four tools in the last 30 days. Logins don't count.

Headline measure

Artists who got a response from another person in the last 30 days: a fan, real link clicks, a play, or a curator opening their pitch. It measures the artist getting something, not the artist doing work.

Data I couldn't trust yet

A dormant tile reading 0% when over half the accounts qualified, track counts that disagreed between tabs, and a column that never populated.

Recommendations

Three tracks, three owners

My outreach reaches one group at a time and stops when I stop. The product reaches every artist who ever signs up. So most of the weight sits with product.

Outcome and validation

Shared for discussion, with targets to judge it by

The recommendation went to the team with an eight-week sequence and targets for each measure, including the share of artists getting a response from another person. It was written for discussion, not yet in motion, so there are no results to report yet.

Verified

The diagnosis, the dashboard problems and the five findings, from the admin data and a live test sign-up.

Hypothesis

That explaining all four tools, routing artists by whether their music is out, and showing them results will lift activation. The instrumentation is there to test it.
